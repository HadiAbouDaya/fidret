# Work presentation and visitor journeys

Use this reference when a visitor must decide whether Fidret's work is relevant to a project. The [design brief](../../../docs/design/fidret-design-brief.md) owns identity and page contracts; the charter owns company truth.

## Make the contribution assessable

Use the smallest summary that answers the actual question:

- What problem or decision is being investigated?
- What did Fidret make, rather than merely describe?
- What does the artifact or result establish, under which conditions?
- Where might this question matter to the reader?
- What remains outside the evidence, and what would further validation need?
- Can the reader inspect the artifact/method or take a legitimate next step?

The summary is a reasoning aid, not a mandatory six-card layout. Keep the essential scope next to the claim. Avoid turning every limitation into a repeated declaration that the company is still developing. A conceptual architecture can demonstrate useful synthesis; a working educational interface is a real interface but not an operating farm or delivery pipeline.

Use a compact worked case appropriate to the artifact. For an experiment, show actual conditions and a result with its denominator. For a model, change one relevant assumption and show the resulting response; link to those exact settings. For a planning brief, show an answered decision from its explicitly fictional example. Read values and outcomes from the canonical source instead of maintaining a second hand-written model. A conceptual preview belongs in a collection; the individual page must add inspectable substance.

Give cards a concise, truthful scope label and a concrete question. Keep the complete limits in the contribution overview. Project-specific invitations should help a reader describe a related situation, and the contact handoff can invite them to include the originating page link.

Work is curated by relevance and evidence. Lab is the publication collection. Do not duplicate the entire chronological collection just to create another page. A contribution overview can bridge a business reader to technical depth while the original experiment/reference remains directly accessible. Published-parent withdrawal must remove dependent overviews, cards and discovery entries.

## Review the path, including direct entry

Inspect Home to work to artifact to people/approach to contact. Also begin on the individual work or Lab URL as if someone forwarded it. Can the reader identify what exists without completing an exercise? Does the primary action say what opens? Can the reader return to the summary or compare work?

Establish relevance before extended examples. A forwarded-link reader should not need to complete the explanation to assess fit. Remove duplicate actions when their labels add no useful distinction. Derive related-work links and contributor promotion from actually published work; test the surviving page when a related piece is withdrawn.

People need truthful context about their role and contribution. A publication count is not a professional biography. Feature only approved information, distinguish personal professional background from Fidret history and preserve actual bylines.

Contact needs a real maintained destination and an understandable handoff. Show what context helps, without requiring a finished specification. Do not invent a company inbox, send a test inquiry, promise a response time or build a form merely to finish the composition.

## Evaluate the composition

Check where relevant work appears and what precedes it, especially on a phone. Preserve visual identity while choosing emphasis from the page's reader task. Original artifacts should explain something specific; do not substitute fake telemetry or generic icon panels for evidence.

Review language for coherent themes, actual contributions and appropriate certainty. Test the entire journey with keyboard, enlarged text and fallback as well as pointer use. Keep summaries and share previews synchronized with what the experience actually does.

An agent critique supports a design hypothesis. Real-reader observations test understanding. Record the difference and use the [validation plan](../../../docs/design/visitor-validation.md) before claiming effectiveness.

## Discovery before terminology

For a first-time reader, name a familiar situation before its engineering category. Introduce “old information” before “freshness”, “permission” before “authority”, and “this version” before “candidate artifact”. Keep the precise term and reasoning in the deeper explanation. Question-led titles must remain descriptive, not become teasers that conceal the subject.

Keep one compact, meaningful project visual available on phones. Use actual source data for values and model outcomes. Scope is visible before interaction; disclosure is for optional depth, not the fact that the system is fictional. Contact needs only an early idea or one example, with other context explicitly optional.

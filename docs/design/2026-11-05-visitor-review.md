# Fidret visitor review and growth plan

Review date: 5 November 2026. Human owner: Hadi Abou Daya. Status: proposals for discussion, not accepted website changes. [Task and verification](../tasks/2026-11-05-visitor-site-review.md).

## Judgment

Fidret currently works well as an engineering credibility reference. It makes careful reasoning inspectable, presents original explanatory artifacts and treats evidence honestly. It only partly serves the larger decision a prospective client must make: whether these people are relevant to a particular project, what they could contribute, and how to begin working together.

A visitor can leave thinking, “They take engineering responsibility seriously,” while still being unable to answer, “What should I involve them in?” That is the main opportunity. More decoration, more broad articles or a new framework would not resolve it.

The website does not need to announce its commercial purpose. Its selection, ordering and explanation of work should help visitors make the decision naturally. A calm, legitimate invitation to discuss a project would serve that experience.

This is an expert review and source-based interpretation, not observed prospect behavior. There is no defensible conversion percentage or numerical effectiveness score without visitor and inquiry evidence. Warm leads can already reply to whoever shared the site, so the missing contact route is less severe for them; it becomes more consequential when the site is forwarded to someone else.

## What already earns confidence

The forest/ivory/sage/copper identity and Manrope typography feel consistent. The sensing illustration and model artifacts are particular to the subject. There is enough restraint for the work to carry the identity.

The newest Labs provide useful interaction: the visitor can change named conditions and inspect consequences. Ordinary reference routes and downloadable briefs support a technical reader who wants depth. Actual bylines, dates, primary references and limitations make the reasoning easier to evaluate.

The static architecture, local assets, shared metadata, published-only discovery, native controls, meaningful initial HTML and interaction fallback are sound foundations. Those strengths should be retained. They do not by themselves establish fast field performance, search visibility or accessibility conformance.

## Where the visitor's decision loses support

### 1. Relevance is harder to establish than the technology direction

[Home](https://fidret.com/) leads with “Building a Physical AI practice” and a company “taking shape.” That accurately describes development, but answers what Fidret is becoming before what a visitor might find useful today. The practice explanation concerns observations, timing and integration, without a concise bridge to a recognizable project situation.

Propose a short explanation of the problems the selected work investigates, followed immediately by relevant evidence. Candidate themes include decisions using changing observations, bounded automation and evidence before increasing autonomy. These are positioning hypotheses, not accepted service offers. Hadi should select the first audience and credible scope before final copy is written.

Physical AI, autonomous farms, agent harnesses and software delivery also need an explicit relationship. Is software delivery supporting the first practice, or a separate commercial area? The site should not silently become a general AI consultancy because those subjects appear in the Lab.

### 2. Work is discoverable, but insufficiently curated

Home repeats the signal experiment as its main evidence destination and automatically features one latest article. The newer interactive work is far down the phone page. The [Lab](https://fidret.com/lab/) calls farm/factory entries “Written pieces,” while summaries still say “Make five decisions.” Their current experience is more accurately a workspace where conditions change modeled outcomes.

Create a curated selected-work overview, either as a Home section and improved Lab entry or a separate Work page if that simplifies navigation. Do not create a second catalogue merely for another menu item. Choose work by visitor relevance and strength of evidence, rather than publication date alone. Present the artifact, its question and what Fidret made. Keep the Lab as the deeper publication collection.

### 3. The work does not summarize its own contribution quickly enough

The visitor can inspect considerable technical detail without receiving a compact answer to what Fidret actually built and what that demonstrates. A business reader should not need to complete five situations or read the engineering notes to understand the work's significance.

Each selected work should begin with:

1. The problem or decision investigated, and who might encounter it.
2. Fidret's actual contribution and the artifact the reader can inspect.
3. The main observed result or conclusion, with its evidence level.
4. The relevant engineering judgment or skill that contribution demonstrates.
5. The boundary of the claim and what further validation would require.
6. A choice to inspect the experience, read the method or discuss a related problem.

For example, the signal piece can explain that late arrivals can look current, that a small guard and replay compare explicit policies, and that the checked synthetic fixture shows the guard withholding age-invalid outputs. Its three-second rule is illustrative and no hardware was tested. This makes the achievement affirmative while preserving the limitation.

### 4. Thoughtful architecture is stronger evidence of reasoning than delivery

| Work | Credibly demonstrates | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal freshness | A bounded software implementation, baseline comparison, failure cases and an inspectable replay | Physical integration, field reliability or client outcome |
| Autonomous farm | Systems decomposition, modeled boundaries and a working educational interface | A running farm, validated twin, robotics integration or agricultural delivery |
| Software factory | Release/recovery reasoning, exact-artifact evidence and a working educational interface | An operating autonomous development pipeline or production improvement |
| Company harness and brief | Research synthesis and a practical planning structure | An implemented enterprise security or control system |

These are worthwhile evidence. They should be described as such. The next substantive proof investment should probably be one narrow implemented workflow with a baseline, failure tests, recorded results and a handover artifact, rather than another large conceptual piece. It may remain entirely software-only. Select it based on the intended lead's problem; do not build a broad platform to fill a portfolio gap.

Authorized prior professional work could also support future case material, provided its use is permitted, contribution is attributed precisely and personal experience is distinguished from a Fidret engagement. Do not create implied clients or joint delivery history.

### 5. People are visible as names, but not as collaborators

[Hadi's profile](https://fidret.com/people/hadi/) lists publications. [Elie's profile](https://fidret.com/people/elie/) currently leads to “No published pieces yet.” Neither explains relevant background, responsibilities or who a prospective collaborator would work with.

Propose approved short biographies, evidence-backed areas of contribution and a clear explanation of responsibilities. A publication count should remain publication metadata, rather than stand in for professional credibility. An empty publication list should not be the main description of a person. Do not manufacture a biography, credential, assignment or byline.

### 6. The approach states principles more clearly than a collaboration

[Approach](https://fidret.com/approach/) explains sound commitments and a five-stage practice-development sequence. It does not explain the practical shape of a first discussion or bounded piece of work: what inputs are useful, what the first deliverable might be, how success is agreed, and how responsibility is shared.

Prepare an example collaboration outline once Hadi defines what Fidret is ready to undertake. A possible structure is situation and constraints, a bounded question, agreed evidence, implementation/evaluation, then a continue/change/stop decision. This is a proposal, not a package or promise. Connect each principle to a concrete artifact where one exists, such as the workflow brief or experiment method.

### 7. Interest has no explicit onward route

Header, footer and work endings circulate among company pages, publications, references and downloads. No inspected route supplies a verified contact destination. A visitor can finish learning without knowing how to involve Fidret.

Plan one real, monitored contact route, with a modest invitation such as “Discuss a project” and a concise indication of useful context: the problem, current workflow and constraints. State only a response expectation the team can maintain. A simple verified destination may suffice; a form is a separate functional choice with submission, failure, data handling and security requirements. The present policy intentionally disallows form submission. Do not insert a fictional address or a nonworking form.

### 8. Honesty sometimes dominates the affirmative account of the work

“Building,” “taking shape,” “intend” and “in development” recur across Home, Approach and Inside Fidret. The limits are often precise, but the overall impression risks emphasizing preparation over completed work.

Keep essential scope limits next to the claim. Pair them with a direct statement of what exists and what it establishes. Reduce repeated company-development explanations across routes. Home should establish relevance and lead to work; Approach should explain collaboration; Inside Fidret should explain people and company context; Charter can retain the full commitments.

## Proposed visitor journey and page outline

The current journey chiefly moves from company direction to technical explanation and then to further reading. The proposed journey should support recognizing a relevant problem, assessing selected work, understanding the contribution and people, then choosing a conversation or further inspection. Deep links must also support that decision independently.

Proposed Home order:

1. A plain introduction to the relevant problem space and current work, with a route to selected work.
2. Two or three curated artifacts, each with a short contribution/evidence summary.
3. A concise explanation of how the work relates to project situations.
4. The people and a practical account of how work could begin, once approved.
5. A real next step, with the Lab available for deeper reading.

Keep the complete charter, source notes and long technical references available without making them prerequisites. Candidate navigation could be Work, Approach, About and a project-discussion route, with Lab clearly accessible. This is a label hypothesis; implementation should select the smallest structure that works, rather than mechanically adopting every proposed page.

## How the design guidance should grow

The [design brief](fidret-design-brief.md) and [project skill](../../skills/fidret-design/SKILL.md) protect honesty, identity and craft well. Their stated reader outcome stops largely at judging sound engineering. They should explicitly support the entire visitor decision. The current brief also hard-codes the signal experiment as Home's primary action, while its surface grammar predates the new workspaces and does not cover selected work, biographies or collaboration/contact.

Proposed opening principle:

> Each primary page should help its intended visitor understand the subject, assess its relevance, identify what Fidret actually contributed, inspect proportionate evidence, and choose a useful next step. These questions guide composition and content selection. The website does not need to explain its commercial purpose.

Proposed amendments:

- Separate durable identity and evidence rules from descriptions of the current page composition. The best primary evidence may change over time.
- Add page contracts recording the intended reader, question, necessary content/evidence and onward route for Home, selected work, individual work, Lab, reference notes, people, approach and contact.
- Require the short work-summary structure above, without turning conceptual work into claimed implementation.
- Add a site-wide journey review and a deep-link/forwarding review alongside component critique.
- Keep prohibitions as diagnostics. Pair them with positive standards for relevance, readable evidence, purposeful emphasis and useful action. Avoid turning “no cards” or a stylistic blacklist into a substitute for judgment.
- Extend mobile QA from reflow to priority: what does a phone visitor learn first, and how soon can they inspect relevant work?
- Add a verification matrix that distinguishes automated contracts, visual inspection, keyboard tasks, screen-reader listening, cross-engine behavior, performance measurements and human comprehension tests.
- Establish a feedback method and review cadence. Evidence about visitor confusion should inform guidance; passing CI or an agent's taste does not establish effectiveness.
- Maintain token usage and responsive patterns as components evolve. Link architecture constraints rather than duplicating changing implementation details throughout the brief.

Suggested brief structure: visitor outcomes and company truth; page contracts and journeys; visual language; interaction/presentation patterns; evaluation. Keep the skill procedural and short. Add a focused work-presentation/journey reference beside the existing [interactive-explanation reference](../../skills/fidret-design/references/interactive-explanations.md). These are proposed amendments only; neither the brief nor skill was changed during this review.

## Testing whether it serves the purpose

Give representative readers an unguided short look, then ask:

- What would you consider involving Fidret in?
- What have they actually built, and what remains a proposal?
- Which work seems relevant to your situation, and why?
- Who is behind it, and what do you still need to know about them?
- What would beginning a collaboration involve?
- How would you take the next step?

Then ask a technical reader to inspect the main claim, its limitation and method, and ask a phone/keyboard user to do the same. Test a direct shared work link as well as Home. Record actual answers and task outcomes rather than merely asking whether the design looks professional.

For later inquiry review, distinguish exposure, relevant-work inspection, attempted contact, confirmed inquiry, qualified discussion and opportunity progression. Manual notes may be enough at first. Do not equate more time on a page, a contact click or a completed teaching interaction with commercial success. Compare future observations with a recorded baseline; low volume will limit quantitative conclusions.

## Recommended first decision

Choose the lead and project situation the next version should make clear. Then curate the existing evidence around that decision, explain the people and the first step, and update the guidance to preserve that intent. The visual identity and technical foundation are strong enough to support this work.

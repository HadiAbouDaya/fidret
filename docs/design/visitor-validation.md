# Visitor understanding and inquiry validation

This is a reusable evaluation plan, established with the [visitor-experience task](../tasks/2026-11-05-visitor-experience.md). It contains no completed human-reader research. Technical and agent reviews must not be recorded as prospect feedback.

## Purpose and participants

Test whether relevant readers understand the work and can make a sensible next decision. Include people who resemble the intended business lead and technical reviewer, rather than only the site authors. Test a phone visitor and a forwarded individual-work link. A small group can reveal directional issues; it cannot establish a reliable conversion percentage.

## Tasks without coaching

Give the reader a brief unguided look. Ask them to describe what they think the company does before explaining it. Then ask:

1. What would you consider involving Fidret in?
2. What have they actually built, and what remains a proposal?
3. Find a piece of work relevant to your situation. Why did you choose it?
4. Find the main result, its limitation and the method that supports it.
5. Who would you work with, and what would beginning involve?
6. Find how you would start a conversation. Stop before sending anything.

Repeat a suitable task from a direct work/Lab link, with keyboard access or on a phone. Record hesitation and wrong turns, not just eventual completion. Ask what evidence would still be needed to consider working together. Avoid asking only whether the site looks professional.

## Record outcomes proportionately

For each task record the entry route, device/access method, unprompted interpretation, completion without help, confusing language, missing evidence and severity. Keep participant identities and verbatim private business information outside this public repository. Shared notes can report non-sensitive aggregate findings and resulting decisions.

Suggested observation fields: date, site edition, audience category, task, result, observed obstacle, proposed correction, owner, recheck. Distinguish an observation from an interpretation. Define success in relation to the task; do not create a synthetic universal site score.

## Inquiry quality

The initial baseline is unmeasured. Use manual private records first if volume is small. Distinguish a contact-link visit, attempted contact, received inquiry, qualified discussion and opportunity progression. Record whether the visitor saw the site, which work mattered and what questions remained, when that information is actually available.

No tracking script is introduced by this plan. If instrumentation becomes useful, design it deliberately with clear purpose, data boundaries and appropriate operating responsibility. More time on a page or a completed model exercise does not establish commercial success.

## Review and decisions

Review after an initial set of relevant sessions and after enough real inquiries to reveal repeated patterns. Do not infer success or failure from a very small denominator. Prioritize a blocked task or misleading claim, then recurring comprehension issues, then refinements. Update the design guidance only when evidence supports a reusable improvement.

Search visibility, field performance and contact receipt require their own service evidence. A working outbound link establishes access to the contact destination, not successful form delivery; a real inquiry must have its actual receipt before it is counted.

## First-time comprehension sessions for the human-centered edition

The [6 November redesign](2026-11-06-human-centered-proposal.md) adds a concrete first round. This is a ready-to-run protocol, not completed research. Hadi owns recruitment and consent. Include at least five non-technical people who have never seen the site: business-owner, operations/process, early-idea and curious-reader perspectives. A technical reviewer is an additional participant, not a replacement for one of the five. Include at least two phone sessions and two forwarded Work-link tasks.

Allow 15–20 minutes on the participant's usual device. Explain that the website is being tested, not the person. Record the actual site edition/commit. Do not explain Fidret or Physical AI first. Obtain consent before recording; keep identities, recordings and private business details outside this public repository. No message or inquiry needs to be sent.

1. Show Home for ten seconds, then hide it. Ask: “What does Fidret do?” Record the unprompted answer.
2. Restore the site. Ask: “What kinds of problems might Fidret work on?” Do not suggest examples.
3. Allow exploration. Ask: “Which project interested you, and why?” Observe first choice, rereading, hesitation and requests for help.
4. Ask: “What has Fidret actually built?” Follow with what the selected example does and does not establish. Look for mistaken beliefs that the fictional farm is deployed or the software factory is operating autonomously.
5. Ask: “Where would you go to discuss an idea?” Stop before sending. Ask what preparation they think is necessary and where the contact link leads.
6. Open a forwarded individual Work page. Ask for its meaning, actual artifact, useful deeper explanation and next step without returning Home.
7. Finally ask whether the site felt clear, welcoming and interesting, and what specifically caused that feeling. Keep any rating beside its explanation and observed behavior.

Use a fresh group for the revised edition's ten-second test where practical. Returning participants can check a correction, but cannot supply a first-visit result. Record accessibility participation and assisted tasks accurately; these sessions do not replace comprehensive assistive-technology review.

### Private observation template

Copy this structure into approved private notes, not into this public repository with identifying information:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Session | Anonymous code, date, site edition/commit, audience category |
| Setup | Device, viewport, access method, entry route, first-time or returning |
| Task | Exact uncoached prompt |
| Understanding | Participant's own words, not an evaluator's rewrite |
| Behavior | First choice, hesitation, wrong turns, completion and help needed |
| Evidence boundary | What they believe is built, fictional, proposed or unvalidated |
| Experience | Clear/welcoming/interesting response and reason |
| Follow-up | Observed obstacle, interpretation kept separate, correction, owner, recheck |

Directional iteration gates: at least four of five independently identify an engineering company, one plausible problem and the conversation route; at least four describe an actual artifact and why it matters; all five distinguish the fictional farm from deployed work after reading its overview. Any persistent false capability belief is a priority correction. These small-sample gates help choose edits; they are not statistically reliable usability or conversion percentages.

Publish only a non-sensitive aggregate: edition tested, participant count/categories, tasks, observed recurring obstacles, evidence misunderstandings, changes made and remaining questions. Never substitute agent reviews, test counts or model interactions for these observations.
